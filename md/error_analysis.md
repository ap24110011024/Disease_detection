# Error Analysis

## Objective

The objective of this analysis is to investigate the prediction errors made by the proposed disease detection framework and identify possible reasons for false positive and false negative classifications.

---

## Methodology

The predictions generated by the trained model were compared against the ground-truth labels from the test dataset.

Misclassified samples were categorized into:

- False Positives (FP)
- False Negatives (FN)

Each category was analyzed to understand the characteristics of the incorrectly classified instances.

---

## False Positives

False Positive samples correspond to patients predicted as diabetic although the actual class was non-diabetic.

Possible reasons include:

- Elevated glucose levels without actual diabetes.
- High Body Mass Index (BMI).
- Family history reflected through Diabetes Pedigree Function.
- Borderline clinical measurements that overlap with diabetic cases.

Although false positives increase unnecessary follow-up examinations, they are generally less harmful than missing an actual disease-positive patient.

---

## False Negatives

False Negative samples correspond to diabetic patients incorrectly classified as non-diabetic.

Possible reasons include:

- Mild or early-stage diabetes.
- Glucose values close to the decision boundary.
- Normal BMI despite diabetes.
- Low separation between diabetic and non-diabetic feature distributions.

False negatives are clinically more significant because delayed diagnosis may postpone treatment.

---

## Observations

The Adaptive MLP achieved a balanced trade-off between precision and recall compared with the baseline models.

The Adaptive + Proactive framework produced higher recall, indicating improved identification of disease-positive patients. Although this resulted in additional false positives, the increased sensitivity is beneficial for proactive disease screening where missing a patient is considered more critical than generating extra follow-up examinations.

---

## Clinical Significance

From a healthcare perspective, reducing false negatives is generally more important than maximizing overall accuracy because undetected disease cases may lead to delayed diagnosis and treatment.

The proactive detection strategy prioritizes early identification of high-risk patients while maintaining competitive overall classification performance.

---

## Conclusion

The error analysis demonstrates that most misclassifications occur in clinically borderline cases where feature distributions overlap. The proposed adaptive learning framework improves the ability to detect positive cases while maintaining a reasonable balance between precision and recall, making it suitable for proactive disease detection.
